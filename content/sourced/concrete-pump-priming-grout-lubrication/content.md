## Why the line needs a film before concrete

ACI 304.2R states the mechanism: pumped concrete moves as a cylinder riding on a thin lubricating film of grout or mortar on the inside diameter of the pipeline. Before pumping begins, that interior diameter must be coated with grout or a specialised commercial primer using a method that covers 100 percent of the wall. Once flow is established with a consistent mixture, the film is maintained.

ACPA’s 23 July 2020 statement is the operator rule we will cite: always prime the pump and placing line before concrete. If you do not prime, the finest cement and water are taken from the first concrete to make the slippery layer, leaving rock and coarse sand that will not pump. Blockages then happen. The optimal primer is a thin slurry containing Portland cement. In most cases ACPA does not recommend water priming.

Pinjin does not print a factory primer recipe, sack count or “first 2 m³ discarded” table. We will not invent one for Electric 15 or Electric 40.

## Blockage is not “push harder”

If the first load plugs because the line was dry, ACPA: do not raise pressure to force a blockage; relieve pressure before opening a coupling. Our [blockage page](/blog/concrete-pump-blockage-causes-prevention/) restates that. A reducer or hose is often where a plug shows first — [reducer / taper](/blog/concrete-pump-reducer-taper-pipe/).

Daily grate and hopper checks stay on the [maintenance checklist](/blog/concrete-pump-daily-maintenance-checklist/). Priming does not replace them.
