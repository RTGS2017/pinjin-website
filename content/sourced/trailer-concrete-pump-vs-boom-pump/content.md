## What this comparison is — and is not

Buyers searching “trailer concrete pump vs boom pump” on marketplaces are usually choosing between a towed pipeline pump and a truck with an articulated placing boom. At our Xingtai factory we manufacture the first family. We do not list truck-mounted placing booms, outrigger spreads or boom lengths. This page maps that search to printed trailer rows. It is not a boom-truck buying guide.

Alibaba’s 2024 pump-machine buying guide groups boom trucks, trailer-mounted pumps, stationary/ground pumps and mini line pumps, and asks whether a truck can enter the site. We use that as a **search-intent observation**. We do not copy supplier prices, boom-reach tables or competitor model names as if they were ours.

## Trailer pump means pipeline, not boom metres

A Pinjin trailer sits on a frame, takes mix in the hopper, and pushes it through pipe or hose to the pour face. Reach is the **horizontal / vertical pair printed on that model page**, plus the pipe you actually lay. A boom pump’s headline number is usually boom length above the truck. Those are different machines.

ACI 304.2R discusses trailer pumps and truck-mounted pumps as separate equipment classes. We do not reprint that manual. The factory takeaway is: if your inquiry says “36 m boom”, we cannot match a Pinjin SKU. If it says output, aggregate, pipe length and power, we can open a trailer page.

## Site access is the usual reason people ask

Marketplace copy emphasises that boom trucks need wide roads and outrigger pads, while trailers and mini pumps fit tighter sites. On our floor that matches why Electric 15 is 450 kg and 1900 mm long, and why we also list a rural diesel pump at 800 kg. If the pour is high-rise and the buyer still wants a boom, that is not our catalogue — see the [high-rise trailer selection note](/blog/high-rise-building-concrete-pump-selection/) for how we read Electric 60 / 80 **pipeline** heights instead.
